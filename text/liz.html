Friday, July 10th, 2:37pm

At the front entrance, the same weathered, handwritten signs I saw nine months ago inform me again to dry off before entering, that if I push too hard I will get stuck on this floor, that this public market is private, actually, not public. I stand outside of Nicole’s one-chair salon just before 2:00pm, next to green tarps tenting the 1980’s tiled floor to catch leaks from the glass-domed ceiling. Neon pylons ready to signal possible slips. Dozens of imported plants make do in these balmy conditions: snake, spider, ficus, rubber, and yucca, all potted in 5-gallon hardware-store buckets. Leggy and errant, moving toward bright objects in the sky. A panicle of purple grapes filters the sun. Nicole greets me, her hands touching mine, to say she’s going to be at least twenty minutes longer. I decide to wander the neighbourhood. Foliage transforms into a lush canopy humid on my skin. I grab an iced coffee on Denman, still thinking about Gathie Falk’s fruit pile I saw at the Vancouver Art Gallery earlier this afternoon. Forty glossy oranges stacked into a grocer’s pyramid. At first glance they tasted more like time than juice. A transitory and indeterminate sensation. Nearing it, I turn the corner and it’s soft gliding tangerine Lip Smackers, and I am laying on plush carpet drawing paths with my little finger, going up market stairs decades later, feeling my way across surfaces. I land at a pair of blue passenger seats with headrests and seatbelts, placed next to a bench with hand-sewn cushions and a leather loveseat. I imagine how they arrived at this food court. I take a photo of this assemblage and receive a text:

Ready
I’m so sorry

We face the mirror, observing growth, and offer how are yous above light blue, tan, and bursts of hand-painted yellow tiles. How was your…year? It’s an impossible question, so we adjust it to the last hour. She adds, I wondered if I might’ve altered yours.

Liz Ellis is an artist, graphic designer, and arts organizer. She works with mark-making, text, and listening-based projects to engage in the poetics of attention, language, and her surroundings. She is currently a candidate in the MFA in Interdisciplinary Arts program at the School for Contemporary Arts at Simon Fraser University.
