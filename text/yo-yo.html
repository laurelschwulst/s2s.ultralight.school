the day before the typhoon came,
i’m lying on the grass facing you,
my face so close to yours
you only see one eye,
a nostril
and the corner of my mouth
pressed into a content grin,
my cheeks pink and speckled,
the cartilage of my ear orange from
indirect sunlight scattering
through its subsurface.
i wear a white fuzzy tank top,
my bare upper arm flaunting
a large dark mole.
my winged eyes
lined black
like those swallows overhead,
their wing tips glittering in the sun.
one flying alone,
gliding and cascading
through the blue sky,
having the time of its life,
adding barrel rolls joyously
with every swoop upwards,
delighting in the ascent,
riding the downs to gain speed into the spin,
then another spin,
then another.
i lie on the grass facing the sky,
wondering how it would be to be a bird,
to fly, to spin in the air,
to catch the gusts of wind and
somersault through.
does a bird have fun when it’s flying?
when was the last time i saw a creature
exude such joy and freedom?
i couldn’t remember.
just like how i couldn’t remember the last time
i laid down on the grass.
this was certainly the first time
i laid down outside with you.
i’m lying on the grass
and i turn to face you.
i stare at how the blue sky
reflects off of the surface of your eyes,
turning them light brown.
you take out your phone
and point it at me.
my first reaction is to tell you to put it away,
but i fall silent,
letting you look at me through this lens.
i like it when you look at me.
you take this picture.

~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*

lying on the grass,
a feeling of calm washes over me.
not a tall building in sight,
just the open horizon and
distant green mountains.
like being on the beach
and lying on the sand.
like when i lived in sunset park
and i would take our blanket up
to the top of the hill in the park
i’d lie down on the grass
and feel the wind from the bay
watching the sun grow pink
and fall into the ocean.
i loved that park.
i walked up the hill,
tired, anxious, overstimulated,

and then after a sunset sesh,
i walked down the hill
windswept, calm, cleansed.
i remember going to that park on
a particularly windy day when i was recovering
and getting whipped around with z,
and we clung to each other for dear life.

~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*

i still think about him all the time,
even when i’m with you.
i used to feel bad about it.
but i’m tired of feeling bad.
i feel what i feel,
and whatever that is,
i’ve decided that it's okay to feel it.
wholly, entirely, even if it’s contradictory.
even if it hurts.
even if it doesn’t really make any sense
nor is what i said i wanted.
it’s tough, but
i’d rather feel something
than feel numb.
i feel because i am alive.
i feel because i have a body and a mind
and everything that comes with it.
i’d rather feel and be messy and difficult,
than be silent and dissociating.
i’ve come to realize that it’s okay to not have the answers.
it’s okay to love you
and also to still be in love with someone else.
it’s okay to be fully myself,
to take the time i need,
to know that the path i am on is the right path
because it is my own.

~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*

these days,
i spend a lot of my time lying down
in bed,
in treatment,
after treatment,
during a dizzy spell,
sleeping.
after my surgery, it was easier to feel numb
than to feel the pain i was in.
i still feel like i am peeling away
at this protective shell i’ve created for myself,
finding ways to safely feel more and more again.
but with chronic illness,
this process is nonlinear,
as the chrysalis forms another hard layer
every week that i have to shed
again and again,
making progress sometimes feel slow
if not nonexistent.
(but like my physical therapist used to say,
slow progress is still progress.)

~*~*~*~*~*~*~*~*

being with you has been like
having a cheat code to feeling again.
i didn’t think it would happen again.
not after z.

i felt immense grief,
as if this one step further with you
was going to take me further away from him,
from who i was,
from that windswept hillside in sunset park.
the distance grew wider.
the knowledge of that was unbearably sad to me.
yet i did,
indeed,
love you,
my heart was swelling
yet simultaneously breaking,
and forming something new.

you laid with me in bed
on the grass
while it happened.

Yo-Yo Lin 林友友 is a Taiwanese-American interdisciplinary artist who uses animation, performance, and sound to create meditative ‘memoryscapes.’ Her body of work reveals and re-values complex embodied experiences, investigating ideologies of healing, care, and disability. She has shown her work at New York Film Festival, SxSW, and The Shed NYC. She is a 2024 United States Artist Fellow.
