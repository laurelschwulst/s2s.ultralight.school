NIGHT

a rock
mat lupine
and a tiny pine tree leaning against a log
caught in the spotlight of my headlamp

two round things
one vertical thing
one horizontal thing

a ready-made still life very much alive

as if
as if in the dark of night I caught the rock and the tree about to embrace
with the crowd of mat lupine as witness

lichen glowing like some sea creature's bioluminescence

fresh green pine tips

there is more to this because I was alone at night walking

full stars no moon

dark

quiet

alone alone alone but not alone

high-altitude alone
deer tracks alone
drifting alone
grateful alone

alone listening for owls
blanket of darkness alone
never alone in nature alone
listening for creatures alone

I have chosen to be alone
bears in the woods alone
light from the bathroom alone
wanting to stay alone

alone with my thoughts
growing and tending and shedding and listening alone
partnered alone
alone on a rock suspended in a sunbeam with everyone else alone

and yet
and yet this is more than that still

MORNING

the aftermath of a dream

washed-out greens in relief against gray and brown
wood chips and pine needles
less showy in the flat light
ordinary without the framing of the spotlight

the green lichen looks different somehow
dusty

the silly potato rock slouches toward the tiny tree
unremarkably

but those
tiny
purple
flowers

little lavender specks in a sea of green
matted flat against the earth
excited to be seen

last night we burrowed under the deep black blanket of spacetime

this morning cools us in tones of daylight

Leah Koransky is a Berkeley-based artist and publisher working in expanded photography. She has shown her work nationally and was a 2024–2025 graduate fellow at Headlands Center for the Arts. She is the author and designer of Glimmer (Deep Time Press, 2025) and Drawing Trees (Princeton Architectural Press, 2022). She also co-authored Memory of Water (Conveyor Editions, 2025).
