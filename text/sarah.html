I’m wading through a lake that is more algae than water. The white dress I’m wearing is turning Depression uranium green. I can’t see them, but I know in my heart that minnows are nibbling on my toes like I know that God is real. The sky is fog, fog dips into lake, Iake dips into me: I’m floating now, gracefully through cucumber smoothie. Out of the corner of my eye I see a sign: “If the lake looks like pea soup or spilled green paint, do not swim. These harmful algal blooms are known as CYANOBACTERIA.” I smile and close my eyes. Whatever can bloom cannot harm me. A mourning dove coos in agreement.
