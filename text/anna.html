Tick Tock by Karin Mamma Andersson

I’m sitting on the couch and my back hurts. In trying to figure out why, I notice once again that I have sat right on top of the gap where two cushions meet, the spot of least support. I see myself getting up and rearranging my body, I see myself feeling better.

I see myself getting up from the couch altogether, planting my feet fully onto the cold marble floor with each step. I see myself walking into the kitchen, turning on the light, boiling water with my kettle. I hear the water crescendo to a full bubbling, I see myself grabbing my favorite mug and making a cup of tea.

I see myself texting my aunt happy birthday, a belated one that I can blame on time difference.

I see myself walking to the bathroom, lifting the plastic bag out of the trash can. I smell iron, my blood from the past week wrapped up in bundles of baby pink and baby blue.

Actually, I don’t really see myself doing these things. I see what I would see if I do these things, an alternate point of view. I feel how I would feel — the firm groundedness from walking slowly, the uncluttered satisfaction of a space cleaned, of interacting with the objects of my home, of moving my body, of using my hands, all of my fingers, of controlling my strength in every decision I make so as to not create a sound too loud.

But on top of all of this, heavier, moldier, greasier, thick, is stickiness. Stickiness from not doing. Stickiness contaminating everything else. It’s sweat that glues you to your shirt, it’s fiber from an orange stuck between nail and flesh, it’s my back hurting from staying in the same spot, it’s the permanent smudge on your glasses and stubborn vegetables in your teeth. I am the vegetable in the couch’s teeth.

I feel a layer of my legs float up and leave my body, moving on its own, away from the mind that controls it. I see myself only in the future that may or may not come, a body that may or may not move. I sink deeper into the couch, I feel bobby pins, hair ties, a sock, lost items found but not retrieved.

A small gecko, half-translucent skin with solid black-beaded eyes, moves only in a hurried pace or a full abrupt stop. I feel the pitter-patter of its steps on the walls of the room that is now my skin.

Anna Kuo is a filmmaker based in Taipei, Taiwan. Her work spans music videos, documentaries, and experimental films. She wields her imagination within the confines of everyday life, probing habits and gestures we take for granted. Her work has been featured by Nowness Asia and Vimeo Staff Pick, screened at ICA Boston and the global climate conference COP26.
